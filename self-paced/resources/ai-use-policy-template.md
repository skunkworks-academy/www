# AI Use Policy Template for CLD-UF-101 Learners

This template is a starting point for organizations delivering **CLD-UF-101 — Claude User Fundamentals**. It is not legal advice. Adapt it with input from legal, compliance, information security, privacy, HR, and business leadership.

---

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how employees, contractors, and learners may use AI assistants such as Claude for work-related activities.

The goal is to encourage productivity and innovation while protecting confidential information, personal data, customer trust, intellectual property, and regulatory obligations.

---

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- Employees
- Contractors
- Temporary staff
- Interns
- Consultants
- Training participants
- Any user accessing AI tools for organization-related work

It covers:

- Public AI tools
- Enterprise AI tools
- AI assistants integrated into business platforms
- AI use during training activities

---

## 3. Approved Use Cases

Users may use approved AI tools for:

- Drafting internal communications
- Summarizing non-sensitive information
- Brainstorming ideas
- Creating outlines and checklists
- Improving grammar, tone, and readability
- Preparing training notes
- Creating non-confidential templates
- Explaining general concepts
- Structuring publicly available information

All output remains subject to human review.

---

## 4. Restricted Use Cases

Users must not use AI tools as the sole authority for:

- Legal advice
- Medical advice
- Financial advice
- Compliance sign-off
- Security incident response decisions
- Employment decisions
- Contract approval
- Customer commitments
- Regulated decision-making

AI may support drafting or analysis, but approved human review and formal processes remain required.

---

## 5. Prohibited Inputs

Unless explicitly approved by the organization, users must not enter or upload:

- Passwords
- API keys
- Access tokens
- Private keys
- Customer personal data
- Employee records
- Financial account information
- Confidential contracts
- Legal privileged material
- Sensitive health information
- Unreleased strategy or product information
- Security vulnerabilities or incident details
- Internal infrastructure details
- Regulated or classified data

---

## 6. Data Minimization

Users should provide the minimum information necessary for the task.

Recommended practices:

- Remove names when not needed.
- Use placeholders.
- Use hypothetical examples.
- Summarize instead of uploading full documents.
- Remove confidential sections.
- Avoid unnecessary attachments.

---

## 7. Output Review Requirements

Users must verify AI output before use, especially for:

- Names
- Dates
- Numbers
- Quotes
- Citations
- Policies
- Pricing
- Legal language
- Compliance claims
- Technical instructions
- Customer-facing claims

The user remains responsible for final content and decisions.

---

## 8. Acceptable Training Use

During CLD-UF-101, learners should use:

- Public information
- Non-sensitive sample documents
- Approved internal examples
- Anonymized content
- Hypothetical scenarios

Learners should not use live confidential client, employee, or customer records unless explicitly approved.

---

## 9. Incident Reporting

Users must report suspected misuse or accidental disclosure according to organizational procedures.

Examples:

- Sensitive data entered into an unapproved tool
- Confidential file uploaded by mistake
- AI output used without required review
- Generated content that introduces legal, compliance, or reputational risk
