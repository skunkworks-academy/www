# Module 1 — Introduction to Claude

**Estimated time:** 30 minutes

**Mode:** Online self-paced

---

## Module Purpose

This module gives you the foundation for using Claude confidently. Before you focus on prompts, documents, Projects, or Memory, you need a clear mental model of what Claude is, what it is good at, and where it needs human review.

Claude is useful because you can communicate with it in ordinary language and ask it to help with work such as drafting, summarizing, analysis, brainstorming, and explanation. Claude is also fallible. It can produce fluent output that still needs checking. The goal is not blind trust. The goal is practical, productive use with good judgement.

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

## 1.1 What Is Claude?

Claude is an AI assistant built to respond to natural-language instructions. You can ask it to draft, explain, summarize, compare, analyse, classify, rewrite, brainstorm, and reason through tasks.

A useful way to think about Claude is this:

> Claude is a knowledgeable colleague you can message in plain language, but you remain responsible for judgement, verification, and final decisions.

This framing matters. Claude is not a magic authority. It is a powerful assistant that can accelerate your thinking and production, especially when you give it clear instructions and enough context.

---

## 1.2 Claude Is Not a Search Engine

A search engine usually retrieves existing webpages and returns links or snippets. Claude generates a response based on the information available to it, the context you provide, and any enabled tools or connected sources.

This difference affects how you use it:

| Need | Better first step |
|---|---|
| Generate a draft email | Ask Claude |
| Summarize a document you upload | Ask Claude |
| Brainstorm options | Ask Claude |
| Find the latest policy, price, regulation, or product update | Use current trusted sources or Claude with web/source verification |
| Confirm legal, medical, compliance, or financial facts | Verify with authoritative sources and qualified reviewers |

Claude can be extremely useful for explanation and synthesis, but for current or high-stakes facts you need a verification habit.

---

## 1.3 Knowledge Cutoff

AI assistants may have a point in time after which they do not automatically know what happened. This is called a **knowledge cutoff**.

In practice:

- Claude can be strong with established concepts, frameworks, drafting, reasoning, and document work.
- Claude may not know about very recent events, product changes, regulations, pricing, or newly released features unless current information is available.
- You should verify recent facts using trusted current sources.

A practical rule:

> If the answer could have changed recently, check it.

Examples include prices, laws, software features, executive roles, product availability, certification requirements, public policy, market data, and security advisories.

---

## 1.4 What Claude Is Good At

Claude is especially useful for knowledge-work tasks.

### Writing and Editing

Claude can help you draft emails, proposals, reports, announcements, policies, job descriptions, and summaries. It can also change tone, shorten text, expand bullet points, or make writing clearer.

### Summarization

Claude can condense meeting notes, long documents, email threads, policies, transcripts, or articles into key points, decisions, actions, risks, and questions.

### Brainstorming

Claude can generate ideas, list options, group alternatives, create names, suggest angles, or pressure-test a plan.

### Analysis

Claude can compare options, reason through trade-offs, identify gaps, build tables, evaluate risks, and structure decisions.

### Q&A and Explanation

Claude can explain unfamiliar concepts at different levels: beginner, manager, technical stakeholder, executive, or customer.

### Document and File Work

Claude can work with uploaded documents, spreadsheets, PDFs, images, slides, or text files, depending on the interface and allowed file types in your environment.

## 1.5 Where to Be Careful

The most important habit in this course is simple:

> Treat Claude output as a strong draft, not automatic truth.

Claude can be confidently wrong. It may state something clearly and fluently even when the fact, citation, date, number, or interpretation is incorrect. This does not mean it is intentionally misleading. It means fluent language is not the same as verified accuracy.

Always check:

- Names
- Dates
- Numbers
- Quotes
- Citations
- Legal or compliance statements
- Medical, safety, or financial guidance
- Customer-facing claims
- Anything that could affect a real person, client, contract, payment, or decision

---

## 1.6 Workplace Scenario

Imagine a manager asks Claude:

> Write a short explanation of our new data retention policy for clients.

Claude produces a polished paragraph with retention periods, legal wording, and compliance claims. The writing may look excellent, but the manager must verify the actual policy, approved retention periods, legal references, and client-facing language before sending it.

The safe workflow is:

1. Use Claude to draft.
2. Compare the draft against the approved policy.
3. Correct any unsupported claims.
4. Send for review if required.
5. Use the final approved version.

Claude accelerates the first draft. It does not replace approval.
