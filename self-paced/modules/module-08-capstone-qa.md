# Module 8 — Capstone Practice and Reflection

**Estimated time:** 60 minutes  
**Course:** CLD-UF-101 — Claude User Fundamentals  
**Mode:** Online self-paced

---

## Module Purpose

This final module brings the full course together. You will complete one real or realistic work task using Claude, document your prompt process, refine the result, and identify what must be verified before using the output.

The aim is practical competence. You should leave the course with a repeatable workflow you can use immediately.

---

## Learning Outcomes

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. Select a suitable real-world task for Claude support.
2. Choose the correct use case or combination of use cases.
3. Write a strong prompt with context and constraints.
4. Iterate on Claude’s output.
5. Use a document or file where appropriate and allowed.
6. Identify verification requirements.
7. Explain how the output will be used in real work.

---

## 8.1 Capstone Overview

Your capstone is a short practical project. It does not need to be complicated. It must show that you can use Claude intentionally and responsibly.

Good capstone task examples:

- Draft a stakeholder update email.
- Summarize a policy or report.
- Create a checklist from a document.
- Prepare questions for a meeting.
- Brainstorm campaign or process improvement ideas.
- Compare options and recommend next steps.
- Turn meeting notes into actions.
- Rewrite an announcement for a specific audience.
- Create a short training outline.

Avoid tasks that require sensitive information unless you have explicit approval. You can use anonymized or hypothetical content.

---

## 8.2 Capstone Workflow

Follow this seven-step workflow:

1. **Pick the task**  
   Choose a task from your real work or a realistic scenario.

2. **Choose the use case**  
   Decide whether you are writing, summarizing, brainstorming, analysing, explaining, working with a file, or combining several use cases.

3. **Write the prompt**  
   Include context, audience, task, format, tone, constraints, and required details.

4. **Run the prompt**  
   Save Claude’s first output.

5. **Iterate**  
   Use at least one follow-up prompt to improve the result.

6. **Verify**  
   Identify names, dates, numbers, claims, assumptions, sensitive details, or expert-review items.

7. **Document the result**  
   Explain how you would use the output and what still needs approval or checking.

## 8.4 Example Capstone

### Scenario

A training coordinator needs to send a reminder to learners attending a half-day online AI course.

### Initial Prompt

```text
I coordinate training sessions for business users. Draft a professional but friendly reminder email for learners attending a half-day online Claude fundamentals course tomorrow. Include: start time, reminder to join 10 minutes early, laptop/internet requirement, instruction to bring one safe work task for practice, and support contact placeholder. Keep it under 180 words and include a subject line.
```

### Follow-Up Prompt

```text
Make the email warmer and more concise. Add a short bullet list for what learners should prepare.
```

### Verification List

Before sending, verify:

- Correct course date and time
- Meeting link
- Support contact
- Learner preparation requirements
- Any organization-specific data policy language

### Final Use

The coordinator can copy the refined draft into email, update placeholders, verify details, and send through the approved communication channel.

## 8.5 Capstone Submission Requirements

Your capstone submission should include:

1. **Task description** — what you chose and why.
2. **Use case selected** — writing, summarization, brainstorming, analysis, Q&A, document/file workflow, or combination.
3. **Initial prompt** — the first prompt you used.
4. **Claude output summary** — short summary of what Claude produced.
5. **Follow-up prompt** — at least one refinement prompt.
6. **Improved output summary** — what changed after iteration.
7. **Verification list** — what must be checked before real use.
8. **Workplace application** — how you will use or adapt the result.

You do not need to submit confidential source material. Summarize or anonymize where required.

## 8.7 Final Reflection

Answer these questions after completing your capstone:

1. What did Claude help you do faster?
2. What did you still need to decide yourself?
3. What did you need to verify?
4. Which prompting technique helped most?
5. What is one task you will try with Claude this week?
6. What guardrail will you apply when using Claude at work?

## 8.8 Continuing Practice

To build skill after the course, use Claude on small, low-risk tasks first:

- Rewrite an email.
- Summarize meeting notes.
- Create a checklist.
- Prepare questions for a meeting.
- Turn rough thoughts into a structured outline.
- Compare options before making a recommendation.

As you become more confident, move into more complex tasks, but keep the verification habit.
