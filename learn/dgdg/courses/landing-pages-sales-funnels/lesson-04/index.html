DGDG-105 · Lesson 4/4

Evidence-led optimisation

Landing Pages & Sales Funnels: Define meaningful tests and guardrails

Lesson concept

Find abandonment points by device, stage and audience; formulate hypotheses; test variables and guard downstream quality. Tiny samples cannot establish certain gains.

Apply the principle to the stated business case before choosing tools or vendors. Define the relevant customer outcome, the information required to support a decision and a measurable way of validating the result. Avoid claims unsupported by evidence.

Worked example

High mobile form starts but low submits suggest inspecting field layout before changing prices.

Practical teaching sequence

  1. Step 1. Choose a primary metric. Record evidence, assumptions, and source provenance in the learner workbook.
  2. Step 2. Define a quality guardrail. Record evidence, assumptions, and source provenance in the learner workbook.
  3. Step 3. Propose A/B change. Record evidence, assumptions, and source provenance in the learner workbook.
  4. Step 4. Write stop conditions. Record evidence, assumptions, and source provenance in the learner workbook.

For each step, include a brief rationale, an expected result and one potential validation method. When estimating marketing outcomes, label synthetic figures explicitly and distinguish estimates from actual observations.

Common misconception or risk: Treating small noisy tests as conclusive. Mitigation: review the evidence, customer impact, permissions and reporting definitions before release.

Formative checkpoint

A relevant conversion guardrail is:

Review the example, select the justified option in the end-of-course knowledge check, and explain why the other options fail the business or compliance objective.

Reflective assignment

Produce 150–250 words explaining what you designed, your highest-risk assumption, how you would verify it and what feedback would change your approach. Cite all external quantitative claims and mark fictional data as simulated.
