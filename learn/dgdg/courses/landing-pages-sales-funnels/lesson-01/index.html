DGDG-105 · Lesson 1/4

Mapping the conversion journey

Landing Pages & Sales Funnels: Map funnel stages and buyer objections

Lesson concept

Define the user objective at discovery, relevance, consideration, action and confirmation. Associate each stage with events, decisions and the information buyers need.

Apply the principle to the stated business case before choosing tools or vendors. Define the relevant customer outcome, the information required to support a decision and a measurable way of validating the result. Avoid claims unsupported by evidence.

Worked example

A course comparison links to the syllabus, then a three-field enquiry form and a response-time confirmation.

Practical teaching sequence

  1. Step 1. Draw five funnel stages. Record evidence, assumptions, and source provenance in the learner workbook.
  2. Step 2. Write stage-specific user goals. Record evidence, assumptions, and source provenance in the learner workbook.
  3. Step 3. Map tracking events. Record evidence, assumptions, and source provenance in the learner workbook.
  4. Step 4. Remove needless detours. Record evidence, assumptions, and source provenance in the learner workbook.

For each step, include a brief rationale, an expected result and one potential validation method. When estimating marketing outcomes, label synthetic figures explicitly and distinguish estimates from actual observations.

Common misconception or risk: Using manipulative popups instead of genuine assistance. Mitigation: review the evidence, customer impact, permissions and reporting definitions before release.

Formative checkpoint

After a completed enquiry, provide:

Review the example, select the justified option in the end-of-course knowledge check, and explain why the other options fail the business or compliance objective.

Reflective assignment

Produce 150–250 words explaining what you designed, your highest-risk assumption, how you would verify it and what feedback would change your approach. Cite all external quantitative claims and mark fictional data as simulated.
