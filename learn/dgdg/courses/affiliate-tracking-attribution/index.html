Affiliate Tracking & Attribution

Design transparent, privacy-conscious referral measurement.

DGDG-103Foundation6 guided hours

Start courseApplied labWorkbook

Professional context

Two webinar affiliates need to reconcile visits, purchases, refunds and eligible commissions.

Measurable outcomes

  • Specify consistent event names and UTMs
  • Compare attribution models and limitations
  • Calculate and reconcile campaign metrics
  • Apply minimal-data and access control practices

Study duration includes independent research, practical construction, review and corrective work. Foundation modules require no paid campaign accounts; practitioner and advanced entries benefit from previous DGDG units or equivalent competence.

Structured learning modules

Lesson 1 / 4

Events and URL taxonomy

Events describe distinct behaviour such as landing view, qualified enquiry and approved purchase. UTM tags label acquisition source, medium and campaign; they should not include names, phone numbers or emails.

Open lesson →
Lesson 2 / 4

Attribution models

First-touch and last-touch reporting give credit to different interactions. Cookies, cross-device behaviour and consent restrictions limit observations. Attribution is a model, not proof of causation.

Open lesson →
Lesson 3 / 4

Reconciliation and ratios

Use named denominators for click-through, lead and approved-sale rates. Distinguish gross events from net eligibility and record reporting windows, currencies and time-zone differences.

Open lesson →
Lesson 4 / 4

Consent, security and access

Minimise personal data in campaign reporting, document consent-dependent technologies, define retention and make role-based dashboards the default. Apply least privilege to partner and payout access.

Assessment policy

Four guided lessons, a practice assessment and a practical evidence assignment support each course. An 80% quiz threshold is a formative readiness indicator, not a certificate. A qualified assessor reviews the project against technical accuracy (30%), reproducible evidence (25%), ethics, security and privacy (25%), and communication/improvement (20%). Proposed practical threshold: 70/100 and no critical deceptive claim, privacy or integrity failure. Learner identity and awards require a controlled LMS process.
