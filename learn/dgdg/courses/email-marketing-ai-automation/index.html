DGDG-106 · self-paced

Email Marketing & AI Automation

Build a permission-aware nurture journey with reviewable AI assistance.

DGDG-106Practitioner8 guided hours

Start courseApplied labWorkbook

Professional context

A training organisation must send enquiry follow-ups without spamming prior students.

Measurable outcomes

  • Document lawful purpose and suppression logic
  • Segment a five-step nurture sequence
  • Design reliable CRM workflow controls
  • Review AI-generated messages for integrity

Study duration includes independent research, practical construction, review and corrective work. Foundation modules require no paid campaign accounts; practitioner and advanced entries benefit from previous DGDG units or equivalent competence.

Structured learning modules

Lesson 1 / 4

List provenance and permitted purpose

Record why an address exists and which messages are appropriate. Explain uses, respect preferences, document regional marketing rules and separate requested responses from promotional campaigns where applicable.

Open lesson →
Lesson 2 / 4

Lifecycle design and frequency

A nurture sequence should provide increasing value, not simply repeat sales requests. Define segmentation rules, wait periods, useful messages, handoff points and cancellation events.

Open lesson →
Lesson 3 / 4

Automation and reliability

Event-driven CRM workflows need validation, deduplication, idempotency, retries, monitoring, human escalation and scoped credentials. Do not embed secrets into student browser pages.

Open lesson →
Lesson 4 / 4

Responsible AI copy and analytics

AI can assist with structure and variants, but humans must check claims, confidentiality, accessibility and intended audience. Monitor replies, complaints, qualified results and deliverability rather than relying solely on opens.

Assessment policy

Four guided lessons, a practice assessment and a practical evidence assignment support each course. An 80% quiz threshold is a formative readiness indicator, not a certificate. A qualified assessor reviews the project against technical accuracy (30%), reproducible evidence (25%), ethics, security and privacy (25%), and communication/improvement (20%). Proposed practical threshold: 70/100 and no critical deceptive claim, privacy or integrity failure. Learner identity and awards require a controlled LMS process.
